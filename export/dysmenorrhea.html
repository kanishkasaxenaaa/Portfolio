Case Study

Designing for Invisible Pain

Inclusive systems design for dysmenorrhea support.

Reimagining real-time support systems for dysmenorrhea through inclusive, accessibility-first, and emotionally aware design that acknowledges invisible chronic pain.

The Challenge

Dysmenorrhea affects millions but remains stigmatized and poorly supported by existing health systems. Those experiencing chronic menstrual pain often face disbelief, inadequate medical support, and a lack of accessible tools for managing daily life during episodes.

Inclusive Design Approach

This project centered on voices typically excluded from health tech design. Through participatory research with individuals experiencing dysmenorrhea, we identified key gaps in existing solutions and designed with accessibility as a foundation, not an afterthought.

Key Principles

Validation Over Judgment

Design that acknowledges pain as real and valid, countering common dismissal in medical contexts.

Accessibility First

High contrast, voice control, simplified interfaces for use during pain episodes when fine motor control is difficult.

Privacy & Agency

User control over data sharing and the ability to communicate needs without detailed explanations.

Systems Thinking

Integration with workplace, educational, and healthcare systems to reduce burden on individuals.

Impact

The inclusive approach resulted in a support system that users described as "finally being understood." Accessibility features designed for pain episodes became valuable for all users in various contexts, demonstrating the universal benefit of inclusive design.
