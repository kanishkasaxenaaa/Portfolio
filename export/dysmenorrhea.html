01

Onboarding & Profile Setup

Pause introduces itself gently. Users select their reasons for joining, rate pain severity, mark body areas, and choose an accessibility profile — mobility, sensory, or communication. A support circle is added, and cycle history is logged to seed predictions.

Accessibility ProfileSupport CircleCycle Baseline
02

7-Day Pre-Period Routine

Seven days before the predicted period, Pause surfaces a Pattern Insight card. It identifies correlations — high stress, low sleep, poor nutrition — and generates a personalised micro-plan. Users customise and confirm. Smart reminders fire at the right time.

Capacity HeatmapPattern InsightsAdaptive Plan
03

Pain Mode — Real-Time Relief

One long-press on the relief button activates Pain Mode. Cognitive load drops to zero. Users are immediately offered a Pause Kit nearby, a guided breathing session, audio relief, or an AI companion. A Pause Space can be found and booked directly from this screen.

Rapid Relief TriggerPause Kit LocatorAI Companion
04

Support Mode — Silent SOS

When users need human support, they select a contact — caregiver, manager, or trusted friend. An AI-drafted, stigma-neutral message is composed automatically. It can be edited, sent via WhatsApp, SMS, or email, and optionally includes a medical validation report.

Silent SOSPrivacy MaskingAI-Drafted Message
05

Clinical Hub & GA34 Passport

Users connect with verified menstrual health specialists for telehealth or in-person consultations. After consultation, a doctor-verified GA34 Clinical Passport is issued — a scannable badge that can be presented at college or workplace as official documentation for leave.

Telehealth PortalGA34 CertificationDysmenorrhea Badge

The Problem

The issue is not the pain itself —
it is the absence of support
in environments that demand
uninterrupted productivity.

70% of menstruators globally experience dysmenorrhea
80% of cases go untreated due to cultural normalisation
79% of our respondents reported zero institutional support
54% push through pain silently — out of fear of judgment
Research

Multi-layered
discovery

We conducted secondary literature review, a 78-person survey, 4 in-depth interviews (including disabled users), and a contextual enquiry on a college campus — building a full picture of lived experience.

01

Survey — 78 Respondents Globally

Distributed via Reddit and personal networks. Average pain score: 5.8/10. 44% scored 7 or above. 15% identified as having a disability. Privacy was the #1 requested feature (55%), followed by heat therapy (51%) and quiet spaces (49%).

02

4 Interviews — Diverse User Types

Tanya (deaf & mute, PCOD), Megha (severe pain 8–9/10), Mansi (banker, workplace context), Ananaya (mobility disability). All 4 hid their pain. All 4 defaulted to going home. None found public infrastructure adequate.

03

Contextual Enquiry — College Campus

Observed a student with temporary mobility impairment managing menstruation on campus. She continued attending despite visible distress, avoided movement, limited washroom use due to inaccessibility, and found no rest space.

04

Secondary Research — 8 Sources

Reviewed peer-reviewed journals (PMC6365059, PMC8943241, PMC9533282), government reports, and case studies. Found only 10% of 119 menstrual apps address pain directly. 25% get no relief from NSAIDs.

"Resting rooms could help, but it still comes with overthinking — how will the manager respond?"

— Survey respondent, working professional

How Might We

The design
questions

How might we provide a person with severe dysmenorrhea with real-time, actionable support to manage pain episodes effectively?

How might we design discreet, stigma-free relief experiences within public and workplace environments?

How might we help users in high-pain distress make quick, intuitive decisions that provide "what to do now" guidance?

How might we create inclusive health solutions that support both temporary injuries and permanent disabilities?

The Solution

Meet Pause.

A discreet, real-time support app that helps individuals manage dysmenorrhea through actionable guidance, accessible interaction, and context-aware support — enabling them to navigate pain with autonomy and minimal disruption.

Phase 1 · Anticipate & Prep

Capacity Heatmap

Predictive calendar visualising pain intensity across cycle days. Paired with a personalised 7-day pre-period micro-routine.

Cycle Prediction Wellness Planning
Phase 2 · Respond

Pain Mode

One long-press activates instant relief tools: haptic breathing, audio relief, Pause Kit locator, and AI companion — all requiring minimal cognitive effort.

Rapid Relief Trigger Pause Kit
Phase 3 · Dignified Assistance

Silent SOS

Discreet emergency alert sent without speech. Privacy Masking disguises the app screen. Caregiver messaging with pre-written, context-aware templates.

Silent SOS Privacy Mode
Phase 4 · Clinical Hub

Telehealth + Passport

Connect with specialists. Generate ICD-11 GA34 certified clinical documentation — a verified medical card usable at college or workplace for leave applications.

Doctor Access Clinical Passport
Phase 5 · Data & Insights

Smartwatch Logging

Passive HRV and stress tracking replaces manual logging. A Correlation Engine links lifestyle patterns (sleep, stress, food) with pain intensity trends.

Wearable Sync Correlation Engine
Physical Space

Pause Space Pod

A bookable physical rest pod: warm indirect lighting, acoustic panelling, reclinable chair, heat therapy zones, and accessible controls. Located via the app.

Wheelchair Access Sensory Design

Outcomes

What this
changes

By centering those most excluded — menstruators with pain, and those with disabilities — Pause creates ripple effects across dignity, productivity, and institutional culture.

5

Core Task Flows

Onboarding, 7-day prep, pain mode, support mode, and clinical hub — each designed for low-effort interaction during distress.

2-in-1

Physical + Digital

The Pause Space pod and the app work as a unified ecosystem — the first to address menstrual pain and disability needs simultaneously.

GA34

Clinical Validation

Users can generate ICD-11 certified documentation — shifting the proof burden away from the individual and toward institutional accountability.

Pain should not be the
price of showing up.

Pause is more than an app. It is a claim that menstrual pain is a design problem — one that public spaces, workplaces, and digital products have failed to solve. Until now.
