01 · Behavioral Finance UX · Mobile App

Fintra

A calmer way to relate to money.

How might we help young adults make more confident financial decisions — by addressing the emotional anxiety that underlies spending, saving, and planning?

Behavioral UXEmotional DesignAI Co-pilotFinancial LiteracyCalm TechMobile App
The Problem

Money is emotional.
Most apps ignore that.

Young adults in India have more financial tools than ever — yet financial anxiety is rising. Budgeting apps focus on numbers and categories but ignore the emotional triggers that actually drive spending behaviour. Most users don't have a math problem. They have a feelings problem.

😰
Financial anxiety is widespread
Users report stress when opening banking apps. Avoidance is common — people check balances less to reduce anxiety, not to manage money better.
🛍️
Emotions drive spending
Stress, boredom, and social comparison are the leading causes of unplanned purchases — not lack of financial information.
📊
Data overload creates paralysis
Existing apps surface too much raw data without emotional context, leaving users confused rather than empowered.
Design Principles

Three principles
for calm finance.

🧘
Emotional awareness first
Before showing numbers, understand how the user feels. Mood check-ins reframe spending from shame to curiosity.
🤖
AI as a gentle co-pilot
The AI doesn't lecture — it reflects. Pattern recognition surfaces behavioural insights in empathetic, non-judgmental language.
🌱
Embedded financial literacy
Education is woven into moments of decision — not delivered as a course. Learning happens through doing, not reading.
Key Screens

The experience
in detail.

Four core flows designed around emotional states rather than financial categories — making Fintra feel less like a spreadsheet and more like a trusted companion.

🏠
Home · Pulse View
Daily financial pulse with mood context. Spending shown relative to personal intention, not raw totals.
📈
Spend Analysis
Weekly patterns with behavioural annotations. AI identifies emotional spending triggers, not just categories.
🎯
Action Plan
Gentle friction before impulsive purchases. 30-second pause + AI-suggested alternatives.
Design Outcome

What Fintra
changes.

Core UX Insight

Shifting language from "you spent ₹8,200 on food" to "your dining has been your comfort this week — here's a gentle nudge" reduced avoidance behaviour in concept testing and increased willingness to engage with financial data.

The behavioural model behind Fintra is rooted in the principle that awareness precedes change. By surfacing emotional patterns non-judgmentally, users develop a healthier relationship with money — not by being told what to do, but by understanding why they do what they do.

The AI co-pilot was designed with a "curious friend, not financial advisor" tone. Every AI touchpoint can be dismissed without friction, preserving user autonomy while still providing meaningful guidance.
