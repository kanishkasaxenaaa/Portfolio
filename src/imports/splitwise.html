Splitwise
Redesign

Shared expenses, without the friction.

How might we reduce confusion and distrust in shared expense management — making transparency feel natural, not transactional?

UX RedesignDashboard DesignCompetitor AnalysisUsability TestingFintech
The Problem

Splitting bills
shouldn't feel like
a negotiation.

Splitwise has strong utility but a fragmented experience. Users struggle to understand who owes what at a glance. The dashboard lacks a clear hierarchy — important actions are buried, and the emotional cost of chasing payments is invisible to the product.

😕
Transparency problem
Users couldn't quickly understand their net balance across all groups without navigating multiple screens.
🔔
Friction in settlement
The "settle up" flow required too many taps and lacked clear confirmation — leading to uncertainty and repeated checking.
📉
Weak information hierarchy
Activity feeds mixed recent expenses with old settled items, creating noise and reducing trust in the data shown.
Redesign Approach

Dashboard-first.
Clarity at every step.

The redesign centers on a "Wallet Mood" concept — giving users an immediate emotional and financial summary on entry. The dashboard now answers three questions instantly: what's my net balance, who owes me, and what needs action today.

Key Design Decisions
💚
Wallet Mood system
Color-coded net balance summary (green = owed, red = owe) gives instant emotional clarity before any numbers are read.
⚡
Quick Actions surface
The four most-used actions (Add Friend, Add Expense, Pay via UPI, Leaderboard) promoted to the dashboard for zero-navigation access.
📅
Week in Review
Contextual weekly summary with streak mechanics reduces the "chasing" feeling by celebrating shared financial habits.
Core Design Insight

Shared expenses carry social weight. The redesign treats each interaction as a moment in a relationship — not just a transaction. Language was rewritten from neutral to warm, and settlement flows were redesigned to feel conclusive and celebratory rather than bureaucratic.
