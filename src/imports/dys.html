Designing for
Invisible Pain

A systems response to dysmenorrhea.

How might we design inclusive support systems for pain that is real but invisible — restoring dignity, access, and care in everyday public life?

Inclusive DesignSystems ThinkingAccessibilityEmotional UXSpeculative Design
The Problem

Pain without
a face still deserves
a system.

Dysmenorrhea — chronic menstrual pain — affects 1 in 10 people globally. Despite its prevalence, it remains invisible in public infrastructure, workplace policy, and digital health tools. Sufferers manage alone, in silence, often without language to ask for help.

This isn't a medical problem that design can solve. It's a systemic invisibility problem that design can begin to address.

🔇
Invisible by design
No public spaces, no workplace systems, no digital tools designed with cyclical pain in mind. Sufferers adapt to spaces that were never built for them.
🗣️
No language to ask
The stigma around menstrual health means many people cannot articulate their need for support — even when they need it most.
🏛️
Systems gap
Healthcare, workplace policy, and public infrastructure all treat this condition as an exception — not a design input.
The Design Response — Pause

A companion for
capacity, not just pain.

Pause is a speculative mobile app and system designed around the concept of "capacity" — acknowledging that pain isn't binary, and that what people need changes hour by hour. Rather than tracking pain as a medical metric, Pause helps users understand their own capacity and access support discreetly.

System Design
🗺️
Capacity Heatmap
Visual representation of personal capacity across a cycle — not just pain tracking, but an emotional and physical readiness map.
🤫
Silent SOS
A discreet one-tap signal that alerts trusted contacts or accesses institutional support without requiring verbal disclosure.
📦
Pause Kit Infrastructure
Speculative physical kits in campus washrooms — accessible through the app — with heat patches, pain relief, and guided breathing.
🏫
Pause Spaces
A policy framework for bookable rest spaces in workplaces and educational institutions — quiet, non-stigmatized, accessible.
🎵
Sensory Mode
High-pain UI mode: dimmed screens, larger tap targets, audio guidance, simplified navigation for when cognition is affected by pain.
💬
AI Companion
A non-diagnostic conversational AI that listens without judgment, provides emotional support, and surfaces appropriate resources.
Design Principle

Every touchpoint was designed around dignity and agency. Users are never asked to disclose more than they choose to. The system works around their privacy, not against it — making support accessible without requiring vulnerability as the price of entry.
